The climax of the single-elimination playoff bracket in Paris delivered an outcome that shattered conventional expectations. Entering an eight-player final stage stacked with world champions and seasoned veterans, Craime executed a methodical run that ended with his hands on the trophy. By navigating past top-tier competition and closing out the tournament on the main stage, Craime earned the $250,000 grand prize and direct qualification to Capcom Cup 13.

## The Run: How Craime Conquered the Paris Bracket

Craime's path through the group phases required constant tactical sharpness. In the opening group stage, he set the tone by defeating veteran competitors like Chris Tatarian. As the tournament transitioned into the second phase, his offensive control with Ryu and M. Bison allowed him to dictate tempo against heavy zoning strategies, earning one of the four playoff spots out of his group.

The turning point of Craime's tournament occurred in the single-elimination playoff bracket. Facing four-time EVO champion MenaRD in the semifinals, Craime absorbed early pressure and capitalized on defensive openings. His ability to punish unsafe space-control tools turned the momentum of the set, propelling him into the final match against Japan's Lily specialist, Hibiki.

In the first-to-five Grand Finals, Craime kept Hibiki's high-mobility approach in check. By consistently anti-airing Condor Spire setups and managing spacing along the screen edge, Craime denied Hibiki the offensive momentum that had carried the player through the lower side of the bracket. Craime closed out the final set to seal the victory and claim the biggest title of his career.

## The Fallen: Top 8 Stories That Defined the Event

Hibiki's run to second place solidified his status as one of the most dangerous character specialists in the world. Battling through tough group stage matchups, Hibiki pushed his character to the absolute limit, taking down multiple premier favorites to secure $130,000 and 750 Club Points. Though he fell just short in the Grand Finals, his performance proved that non-standard character choices remain viable at the absolute highest level.

The rest of the top four highlighted impressive young talent and consistent tournament staples. Fifteen-year-old prodigy Hinao earned third place and $70,000 with a disciplined Sagat display, while Evo Japan 2026 champion Yamaguchi finished fourth, taking home $50,000. Meanwhile, reigning two-time champion xiaohai saw his historical three-peat bid end early, failing to reach the top eight stage.

## The Aftermath: What This Result Changes

Craime's victory delivers 1,000 Club Championship points to Team Falcons, giving the organization a critical boost near the top of the overall multi-game leaderboard. The result also alters the Capcom Pro Tour landscape. By earning direct qualification to Capcom Cup 13, Craime bypasses the regional qualifier grind, allowing him to shift his focus toward long-term preparation for the world championship.

This championship marks a major milestone for South American fighting games. Following in the footsteps of regional trailblazers, Craime's victory in Paris establishes Chile as a true power center in modern Street Fighter competition. His victory demonstrates that dedication and sharp matchup preparation can break through even the most stacked global brackets.

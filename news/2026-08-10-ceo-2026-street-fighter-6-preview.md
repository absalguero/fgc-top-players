He arrives at CEO 2026 as one of the most talked-about players in Street Fighter 6, and the tournament's own history suggests he has as good a shot as anyone to walk away with the belt.

## No Repeat Champion: CEO's Format and What's on the Line

CEO returns to the Rosen Shingle Creek in Orlando for the tournament's 16th year overall and fourth appearance as a Street Fighter 6 stop, running August 14 through 16. The event carries Capcom Pro Tour Premier status, which means the winner earns a direct berth into Capcom Cup 13 if they haven't already secured one through another route. That single fact has shaped every CEO SF6 final so far. MenaRD took the inaugural 2023 title with an aggressive Blanka counterpick over Nephew, Punk broke through in 2024, and Leshar closed out 2025 by beating NL in the grand final after already locking his Capcom Cup spot at Combo Breaker. Three different champions in three years, and none of them has repeated.

The prize pool for 2026 sits at $16,300, built around a $5,200 pot bonus supplied by Capcom on top of tournament organizer funding, the same structure CEO used in 2025.

The bracket itself is standard double elimination. Early rounds run best of three, and every set in Top 8 jumps to best of five games, which rewards players who can adjust across a longer sample rather than those who rely on a single strong opening read.

## The Favorites: Punk and Craime Arrive with the Most to Prove

Punk already has a CEO title on his resume from 2024, and his 2026 season has been defined by consistency rather than one signature run. He finished runner-up to Yamaguchi at EVO Japan after grinding through the losers bracket, a result that keeps him inside the tour's current top five. Orlando is a venue where he has already proven he can close.

Craime's case is different. He made Top 8 at CEO in his rookie offline season and has climbed from there to a 4th-place finish at EVO 2026 and now the EWC title. His path through Paris included eliminating MenaRD 5-3 and Yamaguchi in the semifinal, results that put him ahead of both men on paper heading into August. The question isn't whether Craime can beat top-ranked competition. He already has, twice, in the same weekend. The question is whether a 15-year-old can carry that form through a third Premier bracket in three months without a dip.

---

## The Disruptors: Chile's Wave and the Missing Kings

Craime isn't traveling alone as part of a rising Chilean contingent. Blaz finished runner-up at EWC 2025 at the same age Craime is now, and both are entered at CEO 2026 alongside Dominican Republic's CrossoverRD, who took 7th at EVO 2026.

The bigger absence is at the top. MenaRD, fresh off back-to-back EVO Las Vegas titles and already holding a Capcom Cup 13 spot from that run, does not appear in CEO's full entrant list. Neither does Leshar, the reigning CEO champion, who has spent the summer preparing to lead Korea's roster at the Esports Nations Cup in November instead. Both of the last two CEO winners are confirmed out of the field before pools even begin.

## The Pick: Who Leaves Orlando with the Belt

CEO's three-year history says the field is genuinely open, and this year's absences make that truer than usual. Punk offers the safer bet on paper, with a CEO title already banked and an EVO Japan runner-up finish already on his 2026 resume. Craime offers the bet on form, and form right now means having just beaten the EVO and EVO Japan champions in the same bracket.

The pick here is Craime. Momentum this concentrated is rare in Street Fighter 6, and he's shown he doesn't need home comfort or a favorable bracket seed to beat the players CEO would otherwise crown. The travel and back-to-back Premier grind are real risks, but a player who just solved MenaRD and Yamaguchi in the same weekend isn't likely to be intimidated by anyone Orlando puts in front of him.
